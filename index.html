Data contract, calculation & limitations

Inventory: part, quantity, unit; optional name and category. Purchase: unique line_id, part, quantity, available_date, unit; optional supplier. Demand: unique line_id, part, quantity, demand_date, unit. Use open quantities only. Dates must be YYYY-MM-DD or Excel date cells. Each part needs exactly one opening inventory row (aggregate locations first). Inventory must be available stock as of the baseline start, before demand and receipts on that day. Keep allocations and demand consistent to avoid double-counting. No unit conversion is performed.

Daily projected balance = previous balance + receipts available that day − open demand. The 28-day horizon includes the baseline date. Overdue PO dates are excluded until reconfirmed; past-due open demand is carried into the baseline day. Receipts use available dates after inspection. Demand growth applies to all open demand; receipt delay applies to future receipts. Fractional quantities are allowed; round scenario demand outside this tool if your parts require whole units.

Upload-mode score is a transparent heuristic, not a probability: any projected shortage +50; first shortage within seven days +15; otherwise below a seven-day buffer +20. The buffer equals seven days of average uploaded demand within the horizon. High ≥65, Medium ≥30, Low <30. Missing demand produces Unassessed status. Missing purchase data assumes no incoming receipts. A Low score does not mean every requirement is covered beyond the uploaded horizon. No validated supplier, quality or market-risk inputs are inferred.

No BOM, cross-order allocation, compatible substitute recommendation, server-build impact, live ERP integration, automatic refresh, accounts or cloud storage is included in this version. Confirm source completeness before operational use. Snapshot status changes do not establish that a risk was resolved by a particular action; removed parts are marked not present. Demo product families are illustrative, not orderable SKUs or a compatible server BOM. All demo suppliers and operational figures are fictional.
